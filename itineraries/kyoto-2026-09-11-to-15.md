# Kyoto — Day by Day with Travel Notes

Sept 2026 · 2 travellers · Kyoto Sep 11–15, then Hakone

> **How to read this.** Each day is its own section. **Getting there** blocks tell you the line, the direction, the exit, and roughly how long. Times are local. Fares are approximate IC-card fares per person.

> **Typing into maps:** in Japan, the **English place name** finds things in Google Maps far more reliably than the address. Type "Shirahige Shrine" not "215 Ukawa". Addresses are given below as a cross-check, not as your primary input. Japanese names are included for anything you might need to show a driver.

> **Kansai is not Tokyo — Suica still works.** Your Tokyo IC card covers the Kyoto subway, city buses, Keihan, Eizan, JR West and the Shiga buses. Don't buy a second card. **Cash is the exception:** the Ine sea taxi is paid directly to the boatman, and rural buses and Kibune restaurants are often cash-only. Carry ¥30,000–40,000.

### Getting there — Kyoto → Biwako Valley (~1 hr 45 min, ~¥1,200)

**Leg 1 · Hotel → Kyoto Station** — 15 min
- Tozai line to Karasuma Oike, change to **Karasuma line** south, *for Kyoto*.

**Leg 2 · JR Kosei line → Shiga Station** — ~40 min, ~¥770
- 湖西線 · Shiga Station is **志賀駅** — *not* Shiga**raki**, and not the prefecture name on signs.
- ⚠️ Take a **Local**. Some Kosei-line services run through toward Ōmi-Imazu without stopping at Shiga.

**Leg 3 · Shiga Station → ropeway base** — ~10–30 min, ¥410
- Bus from outside the station. Roughly every 45 min, and **weekend timetables differ from weekdays**.
- ⚠️ **Photograph the return timetable before you ride up.** This bus is the pinch point of the entire day.

### At Biwako Valley — 1547-1 Kido, Ōtsu, Shiga · びわ湖バレイ

- **Ropeway** — 5 min, the fastest in Japan. Green season **Apr 24–Nov 23**, running 09:00–17:00.
- **Biwako Terrace** — tiered infinity-edge decks looking down the length of Lake Biwa. The reason you came.
- **Café 360 and the lounge decks** — eat lunch up here rather than rushing back down.
- **Zip Line Adventure / Sky Walker** — ropes course and zipline, if the day needs adrenaline.
- Give it **three hours**. It's a view you sit with, not a thing you tick.

> ⚠️ **Verify the reopening.** The terrace is closed for maintenance **Sep 7–11** and you arrive on the **12th** — the first day back. Maintenance windows slip. Check [biwako-valley.com](https://www.biwako-valley.com/en/) before you leave the hotel.

> **If the ropeway is shut:** go to **Sanzen-in** in Ōhara instead — a moss-garden temple in a northern mountain valley, ~70 min by bus from Kyoto. It's the substitute the local tour operators use, and it's a good one.

### Getting there — Shiga Station → Shirahige (~40 min)

- **JR Kosei line north**, *for Ōmi-Imazu*, to **Ōmi-Takashima** (近江今津 direction; get off at 近江高島) — ~25 min, ~¥510.
- ⚠️ **Ōmi-Takashima, not Ōmi-Maiko.** Some guides name the wrong station.
- **Taxi from the station rank** — ~10 min, ~¥1,100. There is **no direct public bus.**
- Alternative: **rent bikes at the station tourist office** — 3 km, flat, along the lake. Limited stock.

### At Shirahige Shrine — 215 Ukawa, Takashima, Shiga · 白鬚神社

- **The lake torii** — vermilion, standing out in the water. Shiga's oldest shrine, traditionally dated over 2,000 years back.
- **Main shrine buildings** across the road, plus small stone shrines on the hillside behind.
- ⚠️ **Cross carefully.** The torii viewpoint is on the far side of **Route 161**, a fast highway with no crossing at the obvious spot. People have been hit here. Walk to a proper crossing.
- Afternoon light comes from behind you — the classic shot is sunrise. Treat this as golden hour, not a photo pilgrimage.

### Getting there — Ōmi-Takashima → Kyoto (~60 min, ~¥1,170)

- **JR Kosei line south**, direct to Kyoto Station. Then Karasuma line 2 stops back to Karasuma Oike.

### Dinner — Shokudo Days 🔒

- **330-3 Kawaramachi-dori Shijō-sagaru 2-chome Inaricho, Shimogyō-ku**
- ~12 min walk south from the hotel, or Karasuma line one stop to Shijō.
- Small, fills at both services, **reservation needed**. The omurice is the reason to go.

> **Cut from your reference tour: La Collina Ōmi-Hachiman.** It's on Lake Biwa's *east* shore (JR Biwako line); everything above is on the *west* (JR Kosei line). A bus can loop the lake. A train can't, without ~90 min of backtracking through Yamashina.

### Getting there — Kyoto → Amanohashidate (2 hr 1 min, ~¥5,000)

- **Limited Express Hashidate**, direct from Kyoto Station. **08:38 → 10:39** — the first of the day.
- One seat the whole way, but two railways: **JR San'in main line** to Fukuchiyama, then the same train continues onto the **Kyoto Tango Railway**, which is a *private* company, not JR. That's why the fare is high, and why an ordinary JR pass doesn't cover the last stretch — the Kansai WIDE Area Pass specifically does.
- 🔒 **All seats are reserved — there are no non-reserved cars.** Without a reservation you cannot board. **Book the return at the same time**; it's roughly four departures a day and you'll be taking a Sunday evening one.
- **Return booked: 19:05** direct to Kyoto 🔒

> ⚠️ **Take the ~19:00 back, not the 18:00.** The 08:38 arrival costs you 69 minutes against the old plan, and an 18:00 departure leaves 7h20m on the ground with zero slack — one missed bus and Kasamatsu or the sandbar dies. The later train buys back the hour the morning took. You're home ~21:00 and Monday doesn't start until 08:15.

> ⚠️ **You have 18 minutes to make the 10:57 bus. Go to the bus stop, not the tourist counter.** Ask your questions on the way back instead — the counter is right by the station and you'll pass it again at the end of the day. Missing the 10:57 costs you 45 minutes, because the next is 11:42.

### At Ine — 伊根の舟屋 · Ine, Yosa District, Kyoto

- **Ine no Funaya** — the boathouse village, ~230 houses with their ground floors open straight to the water. From land you only see their backs; the boat is the point.
- **Sea taxi** — ¥1,000pp, ~25 min, **on demand 09:00–17:00**. Ask at the **Ine Tourist Information Center**, 2 min from the bus stop — they arrange it, no phone or Japanese needed. **Pay the boatman cash at the end.** Minimum 2 people, which you have.
- ⚠️ **Take the sea taxi, not the Inewanmeguri cruise.** The cruise is ¥1,200 and its dock is a **30-minute walk from the actual village** — wrong end of the bay.
- **Bike rental** in the village — the waterfront road is flat and quiet.
- **Boathouse cafés** — several converted funaya with air conditioning and water-level views.
- ☎️ **Ine Tourism Association: 0772-32-0277** — call the morning of if the forecast looks bad. Heavy rain or strong wind cancels the boats with little notice.

### Getting there — Ine → Kasamatsu (~50 min)

- Same Tankai bus back toward Miyazu. ⚠️ **Get off at "Amanohashidate Cable-shita"** (天橋立ケーブル下) — the stop for the Kasamatsu cable car, **8 minutes before Amanohashidate Station**.
- This is the whole optimisation. **Do not ride to the station.**
- ⚠️ **Aim for a bus leaving Ine by ~14:45.** The cable car shuts at **17:00** and the ride up is ~50 min from Ine plus the walk — leave it later and you lose Kasamatsu.
- 🔓 **The return timetable is the last thing I don't have** — the table you sent is Miyazu→Ine only. Grab the reverse side at the tourist counter, or ask the Ine information centre when you arrange the sea taxi. Services appear to run roughly hourly.

### At Kasamatsu Park — 傘松公園

- **Cable car or chairlift up** — 4–6 min, ~¥800 return, running 09:00–17:00.
- **Matanozoki** — the view looking at the sandbar upside-down between your legs. This is the famous angle.
- ⚠️ Kasamatsu is the **north-side** viewpoint. The south-side **Amanohashidate View Land**, by the station, has fairground rides and less to do — it's your fallback if you're running late, being 5 min from the platform.

### The sandbar — 天橋立

- **Rent at the Ichinomiya dock** (¥500 / 2 hrs) at the north end, **drop at the Amanohashidate dock** in the south. One-way between the two docks is standard.
- 3.6 km of pine-lined sand — ~20 min to cycle, ~50 to walk.
- **Chion-ji** beside the station — enshrines Monju, deity of wisdom.

> **Why this route, not the obvious one.** Most people bus back to the station, then double back onto the sandbar and reach it around 17:00 as the bike rentals close. Getting off at Fuchū makes it a one-way traverse: north viewpoint, then cycle south to your train. No backtracking, and the sandbar happens in good light. Sunset is ~18:10.

> **If it's raining when you reach Ine:** the sea taxi is on demand, so don't commit on arrival. Do lunch and the cycling first (11:00–12:45), take the boat ~13:00 once showers clear, catch the **~14:42** bus back, and shorten Kasamatsu — or swap it for View Land.

### 💼 Luggage — do this first

- Send your large cases to **Hotel Resol Stay Akihabara, 2-17-10 Kandasudacho, Tokyo** — where you land on Sep 17.
- ~¥2,000–3,000 per case, next-day if lodged before the morning cutoff. The front desk handles everything.
- Travel to Hakone with an overnight bag only. Hakone is ropeways, boats, small buses and ryokan stairs — the worst place in Japan to haul a suitcase.
- ⚠️ Tell Resol Stay Akihabara the bags are coming ahead of your check-in.

### Getting there — Kyoto → Kurama (~55 min, ~¥640)

**Leg 1 · Hotel → Sanjō Keihan** — 10 min on foot
- South to Sanjō-dori, then east to the river. Or Tozai line one stop to **Sanjō Keihan** (三条京阪).

**Leg 2 · Keihan main line → Demachiyanagi** — ~5 min, ¥170
- Northbound, *for Demachiyanagi* (出町柳). It's the **terminus**, so you can't overshoot.

**Leg 3 · Eizan line → Kurama** — 30 min, ¥470
- 叡山電車 · The Eizan platforms are **upstairs at Demachiyanagi**, above the Keihan gates.
- ⚠️ **Kurama line, not Yase-Hieizanguchi.** The line splits at Takaragaike — check the front of the train.
- ⚠️ **Ride to Kurama (鞍馬), the last stop** — *not* Kibune-guchi, which is one before. You walk back over the mountain.
- Sit on the right for the maple tunnel.

### At Kurama — 1074 Kuramahonmachi, Sakyō-ku · 鞍馬寺

- **Niōmon gate** and the cedar avenue up
- **Cable car** — ¥200, 2 min, Japan's shortest railway. Skips the first climb. Walking it (~30 min) is nicer if your legs are willing.
- **Kurama-dera Kondō** (main hall) — **¥500 admission, reception ~09:00–16:00.** The hexagonal *kongōshō* pattern set into the courtyard stone, and the valley view from the terrace.

### The trail — Kurama → Kibune, ~2 hrs

- ⚠️ **Walk it in this direction, not the reverse.** Climbing is front-loaded, you descend *into* the village rather than out of it, and you finish among the restaurants instead of starting there.
- **Okunoin Maō-den** — the inner sanctuary at the top of the pass
- **Kinone-michi** — the stretch where cedar roots have grown out over the path in a web. The signature image of this walk.
- Open year-round except winter.
- ⚠️ **Cedar roots and stone steps get genuinely slick in rain.** See the wet-weather version below.

### At Kibune — 180 Kuramakibunecho, Sakyō-ku · 貴船神社

- **Kawadoko** 🔒 — dining platforms built out over the stream, water running under your table. Kaiseki places run ¥8,000–15,000 a head and want reservations. **Hirobun** does *nagashi-sōmen* — noodles flushed down a bamboo flume that you catch with chopsticks — no reservations, expect a queue.
- ⚠️ **Kawadoko comes down for the year on Sep 30.** This is the single most time-limited thing in your whole trip.
- **Kifune Shrine** — the vermilion lantern staircase, one of Kyoto's best-known approaches. Open **06:00–20:00** through November.
- **Mizuura-mikuji** — fortune slips that stay blank until you float them on the shrine's spring water.
- **Yui-no-yashiro** (middle shrine) and **Okumiya** (inner shrine), further up the valley.
- ⚠️ **Kurama Onsen has reportedly been closed since 2021.** Don't end the hike expecting a soak.

### Getting there — Kibune → Kyoto (~50 min)

- Walk down the valley to **Kibune-guchi** (~30 min, downhill) or take the local bus (¥170, 5 min).
- **Eizan** → Demachiyanagi → **Keihan** → Sanjō Keihan → walk.

### 🌧️ Wet-weather version

Skip the traverse entirely. Go straight to **Kibune-guchi**, up the valley, lantern steps early, a long kawadoko lunch, home mid-afternoon. The gorge is mossy and atmospheric in drizzle — it loses less to rain than anywhere else on this itinerary.

### ☀️ The ambitious version — add dawn Fushimi Inari

The **Keihan main line runs Fushimi-Inari station direct to Demachiyanagi**, no transfer. So this bolts on cleanly:

- **5:40 AM** leave · JR Nara line or Keihan to **Fushimi Inari** (伏見稲荷)
- **6:00–8:30 AM** — the full mountain loop. Open 24 hrs, free, no gates to open. **Above the Yotsutsuji junction it empties out to nearly nobody** — this is the less-touristy torii experience, same shrine, different hour.
- **8:30 AM** Keihan straight north to Demachiyanagi (~25 min)
- **10:00 AM** at Kurama, day continues as above

A twelve-hour day, but the two halves chain without backtracking. Also near here: **Chabako** matcha stand, 13-5 Ichinotsubo-cho, Fukakusa, Fushimi-ku — ⚠️ reported permanently closed, check [@chabako_kyoto](https://www.instagram.com/chabako_kyoto/) first.

## Pre-trip checklist

### Book or buy now

- [ ] **Reserve Hashidate limited express seats, both directions** — Sun 13. Seat reservation is *mandatory* on this train. Out on the ~07:30 from Kyoto (arrives ~09:30); back on the 18:00 direct.
- [ ] **Book kawadoko lunch in Kibune** for Mon 14. Season ends 30 Sept, so you are near the tail. Kaiseki places want reservations and run ¥8,000–15,000 a head. Budget alternative: **Hirobun** for nagashi-sōmen (noodles down a bamboo flume) — no reservations, expect a queue.
- [ ] **Book Shokudo Days** — 330-3 Kawaramachi-dori Shijō-sagaru 2-chome Inaricho, Shimogyō-ku. ~12 min walk from the hotel. Small, fills at lunch and dinner. The omurice is the reason to go. Best slotted as Saturday dinner.
- [ ] **Decide on the Kansai WIDE Area Pass** — ¥12,000 / 5 consecutive days, and 11–15 Sept is exactly five. See "Rail pass maths" below. Marginal buy.

### Verify before you commit

- [ ] **Biwako Terrace reopening after the 7–11 Sept maintenance closure.** You go on the 12th, the first day back. Maintenance windows slip. Check [biwako-valley.com](https://www.biwako-valley.com/en/) before travelling.
- [ ] **Chabako matcha** — reported permanently closed. Check [@chabako_kyoto](https://www.instagram.com/chabako_kyoto/). If alive, it's minutes from Fushimi Inari.
- [ ] **Miho Museum — confirmed closed for your dates.** Between exhibitions; the next runs 26 Sept–6 Dec. Do not plan around it.
- [ ] **Kurama Onsen** — reportedly shut since 2021. Don't end the hike expecting a soak.
- [ ] **Shiga Station → Biwako Valley bus timetable.** Roughly every 45 min, ¥410, and weekday/weekend schedules differ. This is the pinch point of Saturday — check the return time *before* you ride up.

### Thursday 10 Sept

- [ ] **Re-check the Sunday forecast for Amanohashidate.** Read the *wind* row first, not the rain. [yr.no](https://www.yr.no/en/forecast/daily-table/2-7560034/Japan/Kyoto/Miyazu%20Shi/Amanohashidate-eki)
- [ ] If the thunderstorm probability climbs above ~30%, rethink Sunday — lightning is the only thing that hard-stops the sea taxi.
- [ ] Glance at the [JMA typhoon tracker](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/map.html). September is peak season.

### Monday 14 Sept

- [ ] **Send your large bags by takkyubin**, from the hotel front desk, to whatever accommodation follows Hakone. ~¥2,000–3,000 per case, next-day if lodged before the morning cutoff. Hakone is the worst place in Japan to haul suitcases — ropeways, boats, small buses, ryokan stairs. Travel over with an overnight bag.

### Pack / carry

- [ ] **Cash, ¥30,000–40,000.** The Ine sea taxi is paid in cash directly to the boatman. Rural buses are cash. Kibune restaurants often are too.
- [ ] IC card (ICOCA or Suica) for subway, Keihan, Eizan and city buses
- [ ] **Light rain shell** — not an umbrella, which is useless in an open boat and on a mountain trail
- [ ] **Trail-capable shoes** for Monday. Cedar roots and stone steps.
- [ ] Offline maps downloaded
- [ ] Ine Tourism Association: **0772-32-0277** (call the morning of if the forecast looks bad)

## Saturday 12 September — Biwako Valley + Shirahige Shrine

The clear-sky day. If Saturday turns out overcast and another day is clean, swap this with Monday — a ropeway into cloud is a wasted fare.

| Time | |
|---|---|
| 07:45 | Leave hotel. Tozai → Karasuma Oike → Karasuma line → Kyoto Station (~15 min) |
| ~08:15 | **JR Kosei line** to Shiga Station (~40 min) |
| ~08:55 | Bus to Biwako Valley base (~10–30 min, ¥410, roughly every 45 min) |
| 09:30 | **Ropeway up** — 5 minutes, the fastest in Japan. Green season 09:00–17:00. |
| 09:35–12:30 | **Biwako Terrace** |
| ~13:00 | Down, bus back to Shiga Station |
| ~13:45 | JR north to **Ōmi-Takashima** (~25 min) |
| ~14:15 | Taxi to Shirahige (~¥1,100, ~10 min) — or rent bikes at the station tourist office |
| 14:30–15:45 | **Shirahige Shrine** |
| ~16:15 | JR back to Kyoto (~60 min) |
| ~17:30 | Hotel |
| 19:00 | **Shokudo Days** (booked) |

### At Biwako Valley

- **Biwako Terrace** — the tiered infinity-edge decks looking down the length of Lake Biwa. The whole point of coming.
- **Café 360 / the lounge decks** — worth the sit-down; lunch up here rather than rushing back down.
- **Zip Line Adventure and Sky Walker** — the ropes course and zipline, if you want the day to have some adrenaline in it.
- Give it three hours. It's a view you sit with, not a thing you tick.

### At Shirahige Shrine

- **The lake torii** — vermilion, standing out in the water, ~2,000 years of shrine behind it. Shiga's oldest.
- **Main shrine buildings** across the road, plus the small stone shrines on the hillside behind.
- **Safety, seriously:** the torii viewpoint is across **Route 161**, a fast highway with no crossing at the obvious spot. People have been hit here. Walk to a proper crossing.
- Afternoon light comes from behind you here — the classic shot is sunrise, so treat this as a golden-hour visit rather than a photographic pilgrimage.

> **Cut from your reference tour:** La Collina Ōmi-Hachiman. It's on Lake Biwa's *east* shore (JR Biwako line); Biwako Valley and Shirahige are on the *west* (JR Kosei line). A bus can loop the lake. A train can't, without ~90 minutes of backtracking through Yamashina.

> **Fallback if the ropeway is shut:** the tour operator's own substitute is **Sanzen-in in Ōhara**, and it's a good one — a moss-garden temple in a northern mountain valley, ~70 min by bus from Kyoto.

## Sunday 13 September — Ine + Amanohashidate

The long day out. ~09:30 arrival, 18:00 train home.

| Time | |
|---|---|
| ~07:00 | Leave hotel for Kyoto Station |
| ~07:30 | **Limited Express Hashidate** (reserved) — ~2h10m direct |
| **09:30** | Arrive Amanohashidate. **Go to the tourist information counter first** — five minutes, collect the printed bus timetable, confirm sea-taxi conditions. Highest-value five minutes of the day. |
| ~09:55 | Bus to Ine (~55 min, ¥400) |
| ~10:55 | Ine. Walk 2 min to the Tourist Information Center; ask them to arrange a **sea taxi**. No phone or Japanese needed. |
| 11:15–11:45 | Sea taxi around the bay |
| 11:45–13:20 | Rent bikes, ride the funaya waterfront, lunch in a converted boathouse café |
| ~13:35 | Bus back — **stay on past Amanohashidate Station, get off at Fuchū / Kasamatsu-guchi** |
| ~14:20 | Cable car or chairlift to **Kasamatsu Park** (~4–6 min, ~¥800 return) |
| ~15:30 | Down. Rent a bike at **Ichinomiya dock** (¥500 / 2h) |
| 15:45–16:45 | **Cycle the sandbar south**, 3.6 km, drop the bike at the Amanohashidate dock |
| 16:45–17:45 | Chion-ji temple, snacks, souvenirs |
| **18:00** | Direct Hashidate back to Kyoto |

### Things to do

- **Ine no Funaya** — the boathouse village, ~230 houses built with their ground floors open to the water. The sea taxi is the way to see them; from land you only get the backs.
- **Sea taxi over the big cruise.** ¥1,000pp vs ¥1,200, ~25 min, on demand 09:00–17:00, small boat. The Inewanmeguri cruise dock is a **30-minute walk from the actual village** — wrong end of the bay. Minimum 2 people, which you have.
- **Kasamatsu Park** — the *matanozoki* view, looking at the sandbar upside-down between your legs. This is the famous angle. The south-side View Land is the one with fairground rides and less to do.
- **The sandbar itself** — 3.6 km of pine-lined sand, ~50 min to walk, ~20 to cycle.
- **Chion-ji** — beside the station, enshrines Monju, deity of wisdom.

### Why this route, not the obvious one

Most people bus back to the station, then double back onto the sandbar and reach it around 17:00, when the bike rentals are closing. Getting off early at Fuchū turns the day into a one-way traverse: north viewpoint, then cycle south to your train. No backtracking, and the sandbar happens in good light.

### If it's raining when you reach Ine

Sea taxis are **on demand**, so don't commit on arrival. Do lunch and the funaya cycling first (11:00–12:45), take the boat ~13:00 once the showers clear, then catch the **~14:42** bus back and shorten Kasamatsu — or swap it for View Land, which is five minutes from the platform.

> **Bus times were revised for 2026.** Every time above is approximate — the 09:55 may now be 09:26. Confirm at the tourist counter on arrival. ## Monday 14 September — Kurama → Kibune traverse

| Time | |
|---|---|
| 08:15 | Leave hotel, walk to Sanjō Keihan (~10 min) |
| 08:30 | **Keihan** to Demachiyanagi (~5 min, ¥170) |
| 08:50 | **Eizan line** to Kurama (30 min, ¥470) |
| 09:25 | Arrive Kurama |
| 09:30–11:00 | **Kurama-dera** — ¥500. Cable car ¥200 / 2 min, or walk up ~30 min |
| 11:00–13:00 | **Hike over the pass to Kibune** (~2h at a slow pace) |
| 13:00–14:30 | **Kawadoko lunch** on the river platform (booked) |
| 14:30–15:30 | **Kifune Shrine** |
| ~15:45 | Walk down to Kibune-guchi (~30 min) or bus (¥170, 5 min) |
| ~17:00 | Back at the hotel |

**Walk it Kurama → Kibune, not the reverse.** The climbing is front-loaded, you descend *into* the village rather than out of it, and you finish among the restaurants instead of starting there.

### On the Kurama side

- **Niōmon gate** and the cedar avenue up
- **Kurama-dera Kondō (main hall)** — the hexagonal *kongōshō* pattern set into the courtyard stone, and the valley view from the terrace
- **Cable car** — Japan's shortest railway, two minutes, ¥200. Skips the first climb; walking it is nicer if your legs are willing.

### On the trail

- **Okunoin Maō-den**, the inner sanctuary at the top of the pass
- **Kinone-michi** — the stretch where the cedar roots have grown out over the path in a web. The signature image of this walk.
- ~2 hours slowly. Open year-round except winter.

### On the Kibune side

- **Kifune Shrine** — the vermilion lantern staircase, one of Kyoto's best-known approaches. Open 06:00–20:00 through November.
- **Mizuura-mikuji** — fortune slips that stay blank until you float them on the shrine's spring water.
- **Yui-no-yashiro** (the middle shrine) and **Okumiya** (the inner one), up the valley
- **Kawadoko** — dining platforms built out over the stream, so the water runs under your table. Down for the year after **30 September**. This is the single most time-limited thing in your whole trip.

### Wet-weather version

The trail is cedar roots and stone, and it gets genuinely slick. If Monday is wet, skip the traverse: go straight to Kibune-guchi, up the valley, do the lantern steps early, take a long kawadoko lunch, come home. The gorge is mossy and atmospheric in drizzle — it loses less to rain than almost anywhere else on this itinerary.

## Rail pass maths

**Kansai WIDE Area Pass — ¥12,000, 5 consecutive days.** Your 11th–15th is exactly five.

| Journey | Approx. fare |
|---|---|
| Amanohashidate return (Sun) | ~¥10,000 |
| Kyoto → Shiga → Ōmi-Takashima → Kyoto (Sat) | ~¥2,450 |
| **Total** | **~¥12,450** |

So it clears ¥12,000 by about ¥450 — a marginal buy that mostly wins on convenience rather than money. **Monday is not covered at all**: Keihan and the Eizan line are private railways, as is the Biwako Valley bus.

If you'd rather not bother, just buy the Hashidate tickets point-to-point and pay as you go — you lose almost nothing. **Reserve the Hashidate seats either way.**

## What got cut, and why

- **Miho Museum** — closed on your dates. Not a choice.
- **Osaka** — needs a full day to justify, and there isn't one.
- **Fushimi Inari** — displaced by the Kurama traverse. If you want it back, it *can* be bolted onto Monday morning: Keihan runs Fushimi-Inari station direct to Demachiyanagi, so a 05:40 start gets you the empty upper mountain by 06:00 and still puts you at Kurama by 10:00. A twelve-hour day, but the two halves chain cleanly.
- **La Collina Ōmi-Hachiman** — wrong shore of the lake for rail travel.
